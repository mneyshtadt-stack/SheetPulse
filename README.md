# SheetPulse

A single-file, client-side dashboard for tracking a personal investment portfolio and net worth over time. No framework, no build step — just one HTML file that pulls live data from Google Sheets through a small private proxy, behind a Google Sign-In wall.

## Features

- **Home screen** — four tiles (total value, today, total gain, holdings) with today's change split by broker (adding up to the Today figure; while a market is closed its USD/ILS move is shared by each broker's holdings there), and a one-line markets strip under them: TASE / USA open or closed (hover for the next open or close), Portfolio, Israel and USA day change (live while a market trades; click for its window) and USD/ILS (click for its chart). On a phone, today's change is one big number at the top (counts up once; tap for the Portfolio window), the header is a single row with sideways-scrolling buttons, and the build line is hidden.
- **Portfolio overview** — Israeli (TASE) and USA holdings, day gain/loss, a size-weighted treemap of positions (on a phone, tap a tile to see its card), a **Portfolio today** chart under the summary tiles (the whole portfolio through the day against the previous close; before the first reading it shows the last session; click it to open the Portfolio window) with **By market** and **By broker** allocation bars beside it, and a live USD/ILS rate pulled straight from the sheet.
- **Broker filter** — the Portfolio window's header has Broker: All / Meitav / Psagot (the names in the Tracker's column R "Platform"). Choosing a broker shows only its holdings: market value, day change, total gain, value in USD, the holdings table, the 1M–MAX charts and the Pre-Market map. The broker's day change is its share of each market's real day change (from the recorded close; while a market is closed, shared by value, because Google's Day Change % then still shows the last session), so Meitav + Psagot always add up to All; the 1D and 5D charts come from the intraday log, which records the whole portfolio only, so they appear under All. The window always opens on All (both brokers). Each holding's market value shows its dollar value (the Tracker's column H) under the ₪ one, as the brokers' apps do, and the % gain matches theirs (average cost includes commission, like Meitav). **Psagot** shows dollars only; its app's average cost leaves out commission and subtracts each dividend received (after 25% Israeli tax), which a note in the Psagot view and the average-price tooltip explain. To show Psagot's own figures, add a **Broker Avg Cost** column to the Transactions tab and type Psagot's average cost on each ticker's latest buy: Psagot holdings then show "Psagot: −$27.80 · −₪84.88 (−12.72%)" under SheetPulse's gain, and the Psagot view's Total gain tile uses Psagot's way (when every Psagot holding has one). Without it, SheetPulse calculates Psagot's average itself from the **Dividends** tab: `Dividends.gs` adds each US holding's dividends from Yahoo Finance every morning (payment date estimated from the ex-date, 6 days for ETFs and 21 for stocks; correct it by hand and mark the row "manual" from the broker statement if needed), and Psagot's average = purchase price without commission, minus each paid dividend after 25% Israeli tax. A filled Broker Avg Cost still wins.
- **Light and dark mode** — follows the phone or PC setting, in one slate palette: cool light surfaces by day, navy panels at night, a blue accent, green/red for gains and losses (dimmer treemap fills, brighter gain/loss text in dark mode). Text uses the system font (Segoe UI on Windows, SF Pro on Apple, Roboto on Android) and figures the system monospace, so digits line up; nothing to download. Esc closes the open window, and keyboard focus is always visible.
- **Dividends** — the Portfolio window's **Dividends** tab (All or one broker): net dividends after 25% tax for the last 12 months and since the first one (in $ and ≈₪ at today's rate), monthly bars stacked by broker with a hover breakdown per ticker, and a per-ticker table. From the Dividends tab (`Dividends.gs`). It also lists the Psagot averages still typed by hand in Transactions column F, each marked **Keep for now** (the calculation doesn't match yet) or **Delete now** (it matches — with the exact cell to clear); once cleared, a ticker shows **✓ Done** until dismissed.
- **Sector & role-group allocation** — visual breakdowns of what each holding is for (growth, income, hedge, etc.), not just what it is.
- **Net Worth tracker** — assets, liabilities, and pension tracked monthly, with:
  - A hero net worth figure with month-over-month and year-to-date comparisons.
  - Trend charts for Net Worth, Portfolio, Study Funds, and Pension. On the Study Funds and Pension charts, clicking a legend item hides or shows that line, and the chart rescales to the lines still shown — hiding Total lets the individual funds fill the chart. Every line can be hidden; the chart then shows a short "All lines hidden" note until one is clicked back on.
  - A **year switcher** that automatically detects each year's tab in the underlying spreadsheet (2026, 2027, …) with no code changes needed as new years are added.
- **Market Universe** — a sortable research watchlist of tickers with sector, category, and role classifications, plus hover tooltips for the fuller "why I hold this" and "what this fund is" notes. Double-clicking a treemap tile, or a holding's row in the Portfolio or USA Holdings detail table, jumps here filtered to that ticker and shows a floating live-price card (price, day change %, and $/₪ change) above the table.
- **Pulse Check** — a short research verdict for individual stocks, from the "Pulse Check" tab of the same workbook (Ticker | Verdict | As Of | Valuation | Price vs History | Strength 1-2 | Risk 1-2 | Watch Next | Report). Market Universe shows it as a badge column (Deeper look / Watch / Fails, plus Cheap / Fair / Rich against the stock's own history; hover for the details, including what the stock's two labels mean; click the header to sort), and as a panel above the table when the search narrows to one researched ticker. Each row condenses a screen of the company's own filings (the full reports stay local in `stock-screens/`). Research, not advice. No tab = an empty column, no error.
- **Compare** — under a ticker's chart in Market Universe, "+ Compare" adds up to 4 more of your own holdings (those with a column in the PriceHistory tab, e.g. SPY or QQQ as a benchmark). Pick one from the suggestions, or type several separated by commas (`QQQ, VOO`) and press Enter. The chart then shows each one's % change since the first day of the chosen range, one colored line per ticker (a colorblind-checked palette; each ticker keeps its color until removed), with all of them in the hover tooltip. Each chip's × removes it. The list stays while the page is open, so it follows you to the next ticker you search.
- **Live refresh** — open detail views refresh in place on a timer without losing your current search/sort/filter state.
- **Chart ranges** — the Portfolio, USA and Israeli detail charts switch between 1D, 5D, 1M, 6M, YTD, 1Y, 5Y and MAX, with the change over the chosen range.
- **Move bars** — a strip of small bars under the line on every portfolio chart, like a volume strip. Each bar is how much the value moved since the previous point (5 minutes on 1D, a day on 1M and longer): green up, red down, taller for bigger moves (square-root scale, so one big jump doesn't flatten the rest). The cost of new purchases is left out. Hovering shows the exact move. Single-ticker and USD/ILS charts don't have them.
- **USD/ILS chart** — clicking the USD/ILS line on the main page opens its own window: the live rate, a 1D chart from `IntradayLog`'s live rate (vs. the previous close), and 5D–MAX from `PriceHistory`'s daily closes.
- **Ticker charts in Market Universe** — whenever the live-price card shows a ticker, a 5D–MAX chart of that ticker's daily closes (from `PriceHistory`, in USD or ILA) appears under the search row, with its own range buttons next to the card.
- **Installable as an app** — a web app manifest and service worker let Android's Chrome (and other browsers) install this as a real full-screen app with its own icon, no browser chrome, via "Add to Home screen" / "Install app".
- **Biometric quick-unlock** — after signing in with Google once on a device, you can enable Face ID / Fingerprint (via WebAuthn) as a faster way back in on that same device, without repeating the full Google sign-in flow every time. **Sign out** sits under the page title.
- **Connection diagnostics** — the **Diagnostics** button (also on the sign-in screen) checks each link in turn: this device's internet, the server at `api.sheetpulse.org`, the sign-in session and its expiry, the proxy accepting it, Tracker data, how fresh the newest IntradayLog row is, and whether a newer build is live. The first red row is where it breaks, with what to do about it; **Copy report** copies the results (never the session token) to paste elsewhere. Its **Troubleshooting** tab lists the server commands used in past investigations, each with a Copy button. They're grouped by where to run them: **Oracle Cloud Shell** (finding, rebooting or force-resetting a frozen VM, which works even when SSH doesn't) or **PuTTY** (memory, the proxy, Caddy, n8n, ports and updates). A pinned bar at the top searches every command and jumps to a chapter.
- **Pre-market and after-hours quotes** — Market Universe's price card shows a holding's extended-hours trade beside the regular price, in Google Finance's own layout (price, arrow, % and $ change, then "Pre-market · 9:13 AM", New York time). GOOGLEFINANCE() has no extended-hours attribute, so `ExtendedHours.gs` reads Google Finance's quote page every 5 minutes during pre-market (04:00–09:30 New York) and after-hours (16:00–20:00). For any ticker Google gives nothing for, it falls back to Yahoo Finance's chart data; the tooltips say which source was used. It writes Ext Price / Ext Change / Ext Change % / Ext Session / Ext Updated / Ext Source columns into the Investment Universe tab, for each current US holding. Pre-market values are cleared at the regular open; after-hours values stay up until the next pre-market. The Portfolio window's **Pre-Market** tab draws a treemap of just the holdings with an extended-hours trade: box size is the holding's portfolio weight, color is its extended-hours % change. Above it, a summary line gives the session and time, how many US holdings traded, and their weighted move in % and ₪.
- **Winners & losers** — every holding's total return since purchase as a bar. Unchecking a holding (for example one +200% outlier) leaves it out of the bar scale, so the rest are sized against the biggest checked return instead of being squashed. The choice is remembered on that device, and "Include all" resets it. A **Find a ticker** box above the list shows only the matching bars, still in return order and on the same scale. The headline and Analysis still count every holding.
- **AI analysis** — the **AI analysis** button in Winners & losers asks Google Gemini (free tier, `gemini-3.8-flash`, falling back to `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`) for a written read of the portfolio: what drives the return, concentration and exposure, today's moves, and what's worth watching. There's an optional box for your own question. It explains rather than advises: no buy or sell calls.
  - **Privacy:** on Gemini's free tier, Google may use what it's sent to improve its products, so **only percentages leave the server**: each holding's weight, total return, day change, pre-market change, sector and role, plus the USA/TASE split and the USD/ILS move. The proxy module [`proxy/sheetpulse-ai.js`](proxy/sheetpulse-ai.js) enforces this with a field whitelist, so no amount, total value or share count can reach Google.
  - **Setup:** `GEMINI_API_KEY` goes in the VM's `~/sheet-proxy/.env`, with no billing on the project. The route sits behind the same sign-in check as the sheet data and is limited to 40 analyses a day (`AI_DAILY_LIMIT`).
  - **Checks:** Diagnostics shows whether the AI is set up, and Troubleshooting has the install and log commands.
  - **Suggested questions:** three one-click questions sit under the box: what's driving today's move, how much of the return comes from USD/ILS, and which sectors are overweight.
  - **AI daily brief:** a button next to **Daily Brief** above the treemap.
    - **Written by the server** (`/ai/brief`) automatically at **23:50 Israel time, Monday to Friday**, after both markets have closed. It's built from the Tracker sheet itself, percentages only, and saved on the VM in `ai-briefs.json` (the last 7 trading days). If Gemini is busy, the server retries every 5 minutes until 03:00.
    - **Opening the panel** shows the saved brief instantly, for example "Brief for Monday, Sep 28 · written 23:50 automatically", with a picker for earlier days.
    - **Refresh** writes a new one: "so far today" while a market trades, otherwise the last finished session again. It never describes the morning's reset "0% day change".
    - **The day change** in the brief is the size-weighted day change of the holdings themselves, so it always agrees with them.
- **Transactions** — every purchase and sale is one line in the **Transactions** tab, while the Tracker keeps one row per ticker. Tickers are switched over by hand, one at a time; POWR was first.
  - **Columns:** A `Ticker` | B `Shares` | C `Buy Date` | D `Cost/Share` | E `Commission` (per transaction, same currency as the price). Buy Date must be a real date; Ticker must match the Tracker exactly. The dashboard also accepts `Date` / `Price` as header names.
  - **The three Tracker formulas** for a switched-over ticker, shown for POWR on row 37 (change `37` to the row you paste into first, then copy down; every Transactions range is locked with `$` so copying never shifts it, and tickers are compared ignoring spaces and upper/lower case):
    - **Shares (B):** `=SUMPRODUCT(TRIM(UPPER(Transactions!$A$2:$A))=TRIM(UPPER($A37)), Transactions!$B$2:$B)`, the total. A sale's negative shares are subtracted.
    - **Average Cost (D), average-cost method, commissions included:** walks the ticker's lines oldest first. A buy adds shares × price + commission; a sale removes shares at the running average, so the average stays; a sale down to 0 closes the position, and the next buy starts fresh.
      `=LET(t, TRIM(UPPER($A37)), tx, SORT(FILTER({Transactions!$C$2:$C, Transactions!$B$2:$B, Transactions!$D$2:$D, Transactions!$E$2:$E}, TRIM(UPPER(Transactions!$A$2:$A))=t), 1, TRUE), acc, REDUCE({0, 0}, SEQUENCE(ROWS(tx)), LAMBDA(a, i, LET(sh, INDEX(a, 1, 1), cost, INDEX(a, 1, 2), q, INDEX(tx, i, 2), p, N(INDEX(tx, i, 3)), f, N(INDEX(tx, i, 4)), IF(q > 0, {sh + q, cost + q * p + f}, IF(sh + q <= 0, {0, 0}, {sh + q, cost * (sh + q) / sh}))))), IF(INDEX(acc, 1, 1) > 0, INDEX(acc, 1, 2) / INDEX(acc, 1, 1), ""))`
      For POWR: (2×26.58 + 5 + 2×26.74 + 5) ÷ 4 = 29.16. For BRK.B (34 bought, all 34 sold, then 7 + 2 bought): (7×492.83 + 7 + 2×493.23 + 7) ÷ 9 = 494.47. The sold lot no longer counts.
    - **Buy Date (C), when the current position started:**
      `=LET(t, TRIM(UPPER($A37)), tx, SORT(FILTER({Transactions!$C$2:$C, Transactions!$B$2:$B}, TRIM(UPPER(Transactions!$A$2:$A))=t), 1, TRUE), acc, REDUCE({0, 0}, SEQUENCE(ROWS(tx)), LAMBDA(a, i, LET(sh, INDEX(a, 1, 1), start, INDEX(a, 1, 2), q, INDEX(tx, i, 2), d, INDEX(tx, i, 1), IF(sh + q <= 0, {0, 0}, {sh + q, IF(sh = 0, d, start)})))), IF(INDEX(acc, 1, 1) > 0, INDEX(acc, 1, 2), ""))`
      This gives the first purchase, or the first purchase after the last time the position went to 0 (BRK.B: 2025-10-20). Format the cell as a date.
  - **What updates on its own:** Total Cost (=B×D) and Total Cost (ILS) (=Total Cost × Q1, **today's** rate by design), then Current Value and Total Return.
  - **Switching a ticker over:** add its purchase lines **first**, then paste the three formulas. A ticker with the formulas but no lines shows 0 shares. Tickers not yet switched keep working exactly as before.
  - **Buying more:** add one line in Transactions; nothing changes in the Tracker.
  - **Selling:** a line with negative shares. The price is optional: enter the **sale price** per share and the dashboard shows the sale's realized gain or loss (shares × sale price − commission, minus the average cost that left with them). Shares go down and the average cost of what's left stays the same. Selling everything closes the position; a later buy starts a new one.
  - **In the Portfolio table:** expanding a holding shows one row per purchase, in the holding row's own columns and order, as in Google Finance: Purchase date | Purchase price (cost per share, with its commission underneath) | Quantity | Day change | Total gain/loss | Market value | Cost basis (per share, commission included). The purchase rows add up to the holding. Purchases before the position last went to 0 aren't shown; a partial sale shrinks the remaining purchases in proportion. Below them, **Earlier purchases and sales** lists, oldest first, the purchases already sold out (marked "bought · sold later") and every sale with its realized result, so a sale always appears with the purchase it ended. A ticker not in Transactions shows one row from the Tracker's figures. **Total gain/loss is shown two ways** for USD holdings. On top is SheetPulse's own figure, with the cost at **today's** USD/ILS rate (the Tracker's Total Cost (ILS) = Total Cost × Q1, as Google Finance does), so the ₪ return equals the dollar return. Underneath, "at purchase-date rate", the cost is at the USD/ILS close **on each purchase date** (from PriceHistory's USDILS column), as brokers such as Meitav show it, so the ₪ gain includes the currency gain or loss since purchase. Hovering the cell shows both calculations and the currency effect. TASE holdings are already in ₪ and show one figure. Searching Market Universe down to one ticker you hold shows the same holding row and purchase rows, already open, under a "Your holding" heading between the chart and the notes table. Hovering the average price shows the calculation: each line (shares × price + commission, or the cost a sale removed), the total ÷ shares, the average without commissions, and a note when an earlier position was closed out.
  - **History charts:** the dashboard reads each line, so every purchase counts from its own date, its cost is new money rather than gain, and a sale lowers the holding from its date. Each lot's ₪ cost is scaled from the row's own Total Cost (ILS).
  - **Checking it:** Diagnostics has a **Transactions tab** row that lists anything that silently changes share counts: a ticker on two Tracker rows, Tracker Shares that don't match its lines, the same ticker and date entered twice, and shares that aren't numbers. Each comes with its row or line numbers.
  - **Duplicate Tracker rows** for one ticker (a row per purchase) also work: the dashboard and the AI combine them into one holding. [`Transactions.gs`](Transactions.gs) is an optional one-time helper that converts every ticker at once; it isn't used.
- **Always fresh** — every open is a full "Refresh from sheet": on page load, right after signing in, and when the app comes back to the foreground after more than a minute away. Auto-refresh defaults to every 5 minutes.
- **Phone Back button** — closes the open window (a chart, Market Universe or Diagnostics) and returns to the dashboard, instead of leaving the app.

## How it works

The page fetches sheet data with `fetch()` and parses it client-side with [PapaParse](https://www.papaparse.com/); charts and the treemap are drawn with [D3.js](https://d3js.org/). Both libraries are loaded from a CDN — there's nothing to install and nothing to build.

Data doesn't come from Google Sheets' public "Publish to web" CSV export. Instead, the page calls a small self-hosted proxy (a Node.js process behind Caddy, on a free-tier Oracle Cloud VM) which authenticates to the Google Sheets API v4 with an OAuth refresh token and returns the same CSV-shaped data. The underlying spreadsheets are **not** published to the web at all, so the raw data isn't reachable by anyone who merely has a URL.

The proxy is reached at `https://api.sheetpulse.org` — a domain registered at Spaceship, with a single `A` record pointing at the VM and Caddy serving it with an automatic Let's Encrypt certificate. It originally used a free DuckDNS name (`michaelportfolio.duckdns.org`), but some corporate networks block every `duckdns.org` address at the DNS level (free dynamic-DNS services are a common malware category): on the office network the name resolved to a fake address that led nowhere, even though the VM itself was fully reachable by IP. An ordinary owned domain on a mainstream ending (`.org`) isn't caught by that category block. The DuckDNS name has since been removed from the Caddyfile entirely, so the proxy only answers on `api.sheetpulse.org`.

Access to the page itself is gated by **Google Sign-In**: the dashboard shows a "Sign in with Google" screen (via Google Identity Services) instead of any password. Once signed in, every request to the proxy carries the viewer's own Google ID token, and the proxy independently re-verifies that token on **every single request** — checking its signature against Google's own public keys and confirming its email matches the one allowed account — before it will read anything from the sheets. There is no static secret embedded in this page's source for a reader to find and reuse; the one Client ID that is embedded is meant to be public (that's how Google's browser sign-in flow is designed to work) and can't by itself be used to read any data.

Once signed in with Google, a device can additionally register a **WebAuthn** (Face ID / Fingerprint) credential as a per-device shortcut back in. Registering one still requires a fresh, real Google ID token; unlocking with the credential afterward has the proxy verify the biometric assertion itself and hand back a session token, used exactly like a Google ID token from then on and valid for **30 days**. The biometric read never leaves your device — the proxy only ever stores a public key, and only for devices you've explicitly enabled.

**Staying signed in:** the active session token (whichever kind — a Google ID token or a WebAuthn one) is kept in `localStorage`, so closing the tab or fully closing the installed app no longer signs you out — only the token's own expiry does. A plain Google sign-in still expires in ~1 hour (that's fixed by Google, not configurable here); a WebAuthn-unlocked device stays signed in for 30 days before needing Face ID/Fingerprint again. Signing out explicitly clears the stored token regardless of which kind it is.

Intraday price history (used for the IL/US/combined intraday charts) is logged separately: a single Apps Script time-driven trigger bound to the spreadsheet (`logIntradayValue`) snapshots the portfolio's value every ~5 minutes across the whole combined window both markets are ever open (~09:59–23:10 Israel time) into one `IntradayLog` tab, stamping each row's Timestamp column as `dd/mm/yyyy hh:mm:ss`. The dashboard parses that column with an explicit dd/mm/yyyy parser rather than JavaScript's native `Date` constructor, since the native parser only reliably understands `m/d/yyyy` order for slash-separated strings. Every row carries **both** segments' values together — `IL Value` and `USA Value (ILS, live)` — computed from the same `Current Value (ILS)` column on the Tracker sheet regardless of which market is actually open at that moment (`GOOGLEFINANCE` just holds a closed market's last price steady), so there's no need to merge two independently-timed logs on the frontend: IL is flat once TASE closes, USA is flat-but-FX-refreshed before it opens (yesterday's US close re-priced at today's live rate), both are genuinely live during the 16:30–17:30 overlap, and USA alone is live once TASE has closed for the day. A 3rd column, `USD/ILS Rate`, holds the live rate (Tracker Q1) on every row — the source of the USD/ILS window's 1D chart. Each market's opening rate, for the charts' FX markers, is taken from the row closest to its open: at or just before it (the 10:00:00 opening reading for TASE; for USA the row logged in the 16:24–16:30 window, 09:24–09:30 ET, which on Fridays — TASE already closed — the logger writes specially), else the nearest one after, within 30 minutes. (Rows logged before this change carry a rate only on those two opening rows, and the same rule picks exactly them.)

**Missing live prices.** `GOOGLEFINANCE` sometimes returns `#N/A` (or a transient 0) for individual tickers, sometimes for both the live price and the previous close. Those holdings are left out of the rows, so the plain sum would undercount. Whenever at least one holding has no live price, the headline totals fall back to a recorded figure: with **both markets closed**, the latest recorded close (TASE close + USA `$` close × today's live USD/ILS — prices can't move until the open); with **a market open**, the last `IntradayLog` value. Total cost still includes the missing holdings, so `Total gain` stays consistent, and `Today` follows the fallback total. A yellow ⚠ next to "Total value (NIS)" names the missing tickers and the figure used. The treemap still shows those holdings, as grey "no live price" tiles at their last value with a price (kept in the browser's localStorage `sp_lastValues`; else the built-in snapshot's value, else cost); the holdings tables show only holdings with a live price. It's re-evaluated on every refresh, so it clears as soon as Google returns the prices. (If every row is missing at once, that's the sheet still loading, and the existing retry runs instead.)

**Saved snapshot and startup safeguards.** On load, the page first draws the last refresh's data from `localStorage`, then fetches fresh data. JSON can't store `NaN` (the code's "no value", e.g. a `#N/A` day change) and saves it as `null` — and `isNaN(null)` is false, so a snapshot saved during a price outage once crashed the treemap on load. Because that happened before the Refresh button was wired up, the button stopped responding and fresh data never loaded. Now: loading the snapshot turns those `null`s back into `NaN`; a snapshot that still fails to draw is discarded (falling back to the built-in one); the Refresh button is wired before anything else at startup; and any unexpected error shows in the status line under the header instead of failing silently.

`Day Change` / `Prev. close` figures prefer a real recorded closing value over a live approximation. Each market's close is its own `IntradayLog` row, written once a day by the same Apps Script trigger inside its close-grace window and stamped exactly at the close — TASE **17:30:00** (14:00:00 on Fridays), USA 16:00:00 New York time (normally **23:00:00** in Israel) — and filled **green** in the sheet. TASE's close is that row's `IL Value`; USA's close is its `USA Value (ILS, live)`, `USA Value ($)` and `USD/ILS Rate`. The dashboard reads those rows directly (if a close row was ever missed, the last row before the close stands in), so a number and its "as of" timestamp always describe the same real moment. This replaced a separate `LastClose` tab: `IntradayLog` already keeps the same 7 trading days, and its new `USA Value ($)` column (USA's holdings in dollars, from the Tracker's `Current Value ($)`) was the only thing `LastClose` had on top. If no recorded close is available at all, it falls back to summing each holding's own live day-change %.

`Prev. close` always uses the most recent close **before today**, even after today's own close rows exist — otherwise, the moment a market's close gets written, it would compare today's final value against itself (`+₪0 / 0.00%`) instead of staying anchored to the prior session until a new calendar day begins.

Because `USA market`'s ILS figure is fixed at whatever the rate was at that past close, it doesn't track today's live USD/ILS movement — the USA and combined `Day Change` tiles show that recorded rate directly (e.g. "at USD/ILS 3.0221") next to `Prev. close`, so it's clear which rate the number is anchored to rather than silently comparing today's value against a stale-rate figure with no indication.

The combined (Portfolio) chart's pre-open stretch (TASE trading alone, before USA opens) reflects real FX movement rather than holding USA flat at yesterday's ILS figure: `USA Value (ILS, live)` is genuine live per-tick data from `IntradayLog` for that whole stretch (yesterday's US close, continuously re-priced at whatever USD/ILS rate is live on the Tracker sheet at each tick), not a one-time estimate. A flat stand-in (yesterday's US close re-priced at the ~09:59 captured rate) is only ever used as a fallback, for the rare case a row's own USA column comes back blank.

Both the Portfolio chart (at its own ~10:00 opening point) and the USA-only chart (at its own ~16:30 opening point) carry a small marker highlighting the FX move baked into that chart's opening number: a solid accent-colored dot, and a tooltip on hover showing the rate at that open (from the row closest to 10:00 for Portfolio, to 16:30 for USA) against the previous close's own recorded rate, with the change in percent. This only affects the chart's plotted marker/tooltip — the `Prev. close`/`Day Change` header figures stay anchored to the real historical close on purpose, so they never disagree with their own label.

**The USA-only chart's axis covers USA's own real session** — 9:30–16:00 ET (~16:30–23:00 Israel time). A brief experiment with starting it at TASE's 10:00 open was reverted: until USA actually opens, the USA chart instead shows its last completed session (see below), which is the more useful thing to look at during the TASE-only morning.

When today's own trading window has fewer than 2 logged points yet (before that market opens, or a weekend/holiday), each intraday chart (IL, USA, Portfolio) falls back to replaying the most recently completed session instead of showing an empty chart — walking backward, day by day, through the distinct calendar dates actually present in `IntradayLog` until it finds one with real data. Once a market is **open**, its chart switches to today from the first point, even a single one (drawn as a dot until the next reading arrives). Every weekday between 09:54 and 10:00 the logger writes an opening reading stamped exactly **10:00:00** — TASE prices can't move before the open, so it's the true opening value, with USA re-priced at the live USD/ILS — so the TASE and Portfolio charts have a point the moment TASE opens. If that reading was missed, the last value logged before the open stands in as the opening point (USA's ~16:25 row, or the previous session's last row, looking back up to 4 days for weekends). While a chart is still waiting for today's data during market hours it re-checks every minute instead of waiting for the window's 5-minute refresh. Before the open, the chart keeps showing the last session. `IntradayLog` keeps the **7 most recent distinct trading dates** (`INTRADAY_KEEP_DATES` in `IntradayLogging.gs`): the fallback only needs the previous one, and the 5D chart range (below) needs five, plus a margin in case the trigger misses a whole day's runs. Dates are counted as they appear in the sheet, so weekends need no special-casing — nothing logs on Saturday/Sunday, so Friday stays through all of Monday. A replayed session shows its *own* correct figures rather than today's:
- **`Prev. close`** — e.g. Monday's close when replaying Tuesday — from the full per-date history of recorded closes (`window.lastCloseByDate`, every close row in `IntradayLog`'s 7 days, not just the most recent): the closest recorded close strictly before the replayed date.
- **The FX-rate opening marker** — that session's own captured opening rate (`window.intradayOpenRateByDate`, collected from every day's rows in `IntradayLog`, not just today's) against the closing rate of the trading day before it (`findPriorFxRate`, from the same close history).
- **Its closing value** — shown on the chart itself as a labeled "Close ₪…" callout with an enlarged end-point dot, since the header tiles above show today's live-current figure, which can genuinely differ from that past session's frozen close once FX has moved.

**Chart ranges.** Under each detail chart (Portfolio, USA, Israeli), buttons switch between **1D · 5D · 1M · 6M · YTD · 1Y · 5Y · MAX**. The choice survives the window's auto-refresh and resets to 1D when a window is opened. Above the chart: the change over the range (e.g. "▲ +₪12,345 (+1.93%) past month"); the dashed line marks the range's start value, and hovering a point shows its value and change vs. the start. Holdings bought after the range's first day add their purchase cost to the value — new money, not gain — so the change subtracts their Tracker `Total Cost (ILS)` and the percentage is the gain on start value + new money ("· excludes ₪N of new purchases"). Without that, MAX would show thousands of percent from a tiny first holding. 5Y and MAX are identical until the history reaches back more than 5 years (it starts at the earliest `Buy Date`).
- **1D** is the intraday chart described above.
- **5D** replots `IntradayLog`'s last 5 trading dates, each clipped to that view's own trading hours and spaced evenly by point, so nights don't leave gaps.
- **1M and longer** come from the `PriceHistory` tab: each day's value is the current share count × that day's close × that day's USD/ILS rate (agorot ÷ 100 for the TASE funds), counting each holding only from its own `Buy Date`; the last point is today's live value. Because it uses *current* share counts, positions since sold don't appear and repeat purchases of one ticker count from the Tracker's single `Buy Date` — the chart says so under it. A new purchase shows up as a step up on its buy date. If a ticker's latest history close is more than 30% away from the Tracker's current price, its `History` symbol has almost certainly resolved to a different listing (another exchange or currency — e.g. a bare `COWZ` returned ~3,047 against the Tracker's $68); that ticker is left out of the history and named under the chart, and the fix is an exchange prefix in `History` (`BATS:COWZ`).

**`History` and `PriceHistory` tabs.** `History` is a formula tab edited by hand like the Tracker: `B1` is the start date, row 3 holds one symbol per column pair (`CURRENCY:USDILS`, then every Market Universe ticker), and row 4 holds `=QUERY(GOOGLEFINANCE(symbol,"close",$B$1,TODAY(),"DAILY"),"select * order by Col1 desc",1)` under each, spilling Date | Close newest date first (`GOOGLEFINANCE` itself returns oldest-first; `convertHistoryToDescending` wraps existing plain formulas once). The dashboard can't read it directly: through the Sheets API, which the proxy uses, `GOOGLEFINANCE` history comes back as `#N/A` for US-listed tickers (the TASE funds happen to come through). Apps Script *can* read it, so `copyHistoryToPriceHistory` in `PriceHistory.gs` copies the values every morning at 07:00 into `PriceHistory` — `Date` (`yyyy-mm-dd` text) | `USDILS` | one column per ticker, newest date first, prices in each ticker's own quote currency. The dashboard sorts it by date itself, so it doesn't depend on the tab's row order. The copy merges rather than overwrites, so a block that's temporarily `#N/A` never erases earlier values, and an exchange prefix like `NYSEARCA:VOO` is stored as plain `VOO`. `createHistoryTab` builds a starter `History` tab once, from the earliest Tracker `Buy Date`.

Both the USA-only and combined (Portfolio) chart's "today" is deliberately anchored to **Israel's own calendar date**, matching the TASE chart, rather than New York's local date. During Israel's early-morning hours (roughly midnight–7am), New York is still on the previous date — anchoring to NY's date there would silently point "today" at yesterday's already-completed session, which then matched the raw data directly and skipped the fallback-and-historical-`Prev.-close` logic above entirely, showing a correct-looking chart next to a self-referencing (0%) `Prev. close`.

## Day Change and currency movement

`Day Change` deliberately includes USD/ILS currency movement, not just each holding's own price movement — this is a considered design choice, not an oversight.

Your net worth is denominated in shekels; that's the currency you'll actually spend. If a US stock's price doesn't move at all but USD strengthens 1% against ILS, you really are 1% richer in ILS terms today — that's real, realizable wealth change, not a measurement quirk. Comparing today's `$price × today's rate` against yesterday's `$price × yesterday's rate` (what the recorded close + the live rate does) correctly captures the combined effect of stock performance and currency movement, which is exactly what "how much richer/poorer am I today" has to mean for an ILS-based investor.

This is a different convention from Google Finance's own per-security `change`/`changepct` (the numbers behind the Holdings table's `Day Change (%)` column), which compares a security's price only against its own previous local-currency close and never factors in FX at all — the right number for judging a stock's own performance on its own exchange, but the wrong one for a portfolio's real ILS-denominated day-over-day change. SheetPulse deliberately uses two different conventions for two different questions: the per-ticker table answers "how did this stock do," the aggregate header answers "how did my wealth change." The FX-rate markers on the intraday charts (see above) exist specifically to make the currency-driven portion of that second number visible, rather than leaving it silently blended into one figure.

**Bottom line — what's actually being compared:** every `Day Change` figure (header stats, the intraday chart's `Prev. close` reference line, and the FX markers) is *today's live ILS value* minus *the recorded close's ILS value*. The recorded side is a real historical figure, written once by Apps Script at the moment each market actually closed and never recalculated afterward — it was already `$price × that day's closing rate` at the instant it was written, not something reconstructed later from separate price/rate columns. The live side always uses whatever rate is live right now. Because one side is frozen at a past rate and the other moves with today's rate, the gap between them is, by construction, the combined effect of price movement and FX movement — there's no separate "FX adjustment" applied anywhere; it falls out of comparing a frozen number to a live one.

**The `Market Value (USD)` tiles are a different, deliberately narrower question** — "how did the stock(s) themselves do, in their own currency" — so they're built to be as FX-clean as possible rather than FX-inclusive:
- **USA-only view:** today's $ value is the live sum of each holding's own `Current Value ($)` (Tracker column H, real per-ticker dollar data, not derived by dividing the ILS total through today's rate), compared against the `USA Value ($)` recorded at the previous close — a genuine dollars-to-dollars comparison with no currency movement mixed in at all.
- **Portfolio (combined) view:** since TASE holdings have no real independent dollar identity — their "$" figure only ever exists as a function of *some* exchange rate — the combined tile splits into its two segments rather than dividing the whole ILS total by one rate: today's total is `(IL's ILS value ÷ today's live rate) + USA's real $ value`, and the reference side is `(the recorded IL close ÷ that close's own recorded rate) + the real USA $ value recorded at the close` — anchoring IL's unavoidable FX component to a frozen historical rate instead of today's live one, while USA's half stays fully FX-clean. This can never be as clean as the USA-only tile (there's no way around IL's FX dependency), but it removes the live-rate drift the naive whole-total-divided-by-today's-rate approach had.

Both tiles fall back to the simpler derived estimate (dividing the ILS total through today's live rate) whenever the real underlying data — `Current Value ($)`, or the recorded close's `USA Value ($)`/rate — isn't available yet for that day.

## Bank of Israel rate

Meitav values its US holdings at the **Bank of Israel representative rate** (שער יציג), and Psagot measures its ₪ day change from the latest representative rate before today. SheetPulse reads those rates from a **BOIRate** tab in the Tracker spreadsheet: add a tab named exactly `BOIRate` and put this one formula in A1:

```
=QUERY(IMPORTDATA("https://edge.boi.gov.il/FusionEdgeServer/sdmx/v2/data/dataflow/BOI.STATISTICS/EXR/1.0/RER_USD_ILS?startperiod="&TEXT(TODAY()-30,"yyyy-mm-dd")&"&format=csv"),"select * order by Col13 desc",1)
```

It fills in the last 30 days of rates, newest first (columns TIME_PERIOD and OBS_VALUE; format column M as a date to read it) and Google refreshes it about every hour; the Bank of Israel publishes each day's rate around 15:30 Israel time. No script or trigger is needed. With the tab in place:

- **Meitav view:** the Total gain tile and each holding's second gain line ("Meitav (purchase-date rate)") are Meitav's way: US holdings valued at the latest representative rate, cost at the USD/ILS close on each purchase date. Hovering shows the rate and its date.
- **Psagot view:** the Day change tile is Psagot's way: today's ₪ value minus yesterday's dollar value at the latest representative rate before today. Before the US session opens, the dollar move is $0 (Google's Day Change % still shows the last session then), so the day change is only the rate move, as in Psagot's app. Each Psagot holding's $ day change is $0 until the US opens, too.
- **USD/ILS:** the markets strip and the USD/ILS window's headline show the latest rate and its daily change, as the Bank of Israel shows them ("BoI 3.0530 ↓-0.196% (Oct 6)"), next to the live one. On the chart the rates show as blue ◆ points: on 1D, today's rate at 15:30 with a dotted line from it to the right edge, like Prev. close (before it is published, a note gives the latest one); on longer ranges, one per day next to Google's close, also in the hover.
- Without the tab, both views show SheetPulse's own figures, and Diagnostics → "Bank of Israel rate" says so.

## Setup

This dashboard is wired to one specific Google account's sheets and one allowed sign-in email via hardcoded constants in the HTML (`SHEET_ID`, `NET_WORTH_SHEET_ID`, `PROXY_BASE_URL`, `GOOGLE_WEB_CLIENT_ID`, `ALLOWED_GOOGLE_EMAIL`) — it isn't meant to be reconfigured per-viewer. To point it at your own sheets and account:

1. Stand up the proxy: a small Node.js server (using the `googleapis` package with an OAuth2 client + refresh token for Sheets access, plus `google-auth-library` to verify incoming sign-ins) behind a reverse proxy with HTTPS, reachable at some `PROXY_BASE_URL`. It exposes `?sheetId=...&sheetName=...` (returns that tab as CSV) and `?sheetId=...&action=list` (returns all tab names — used for the Net Worth year switcher).
2. Create two OAuth clients in Google Cloud: a **Desktop app** client (for the proxy's own read access to your Sheets, via a one-time refresh-token exchange) and a **Web application** client (for Google Sign-In on the page itself), with the page's own origin listed under its Authorized JavaScript origins.
3. Update the `SHEET_ID` / `NET_WORTH_SHEET_ID` constants in the HTML to your own spreadsheet IDs (from each sheet's normal `.../spreadsheets/d/<ID>/edit` URL — the file must be in native Google Sheets format, not an uploaded Excel file).
4. Set `GOOGLE_WEB_CLIENT_ID` in the HTML to the Web client's ID, and set `ALLOWED_GOOGLE_EMAIL` to the Google account that should be allowed to sign in. On the proxy, set matching `WEB_CLIENT_ID` and `ALLOWED_EMAIL` values so it can independently verify the same thing server-side, and restrict its CORS to the origin you're hosting this page on.

## Local testing

`SheetPulse.html` is always the source of truth — it's the one file that ever gets edited by hand and the one file the deploy script reads. To try a change before it goes live:

1. Edit `SheetPulse.html`.
2. Run `TestLocal.bat` — it copies `SheetPulse.html` to `TestLocal.html` fresh (so the test copy can never drift out of sync), starts a local server, and opens `http://localhost:8000/TestLocal.html`. The URL itself makes it obvious you're looking at the local test copy, not production. `TestLocal.html` is disposable and gets overwritten every run — never edit it directly, since those changes would be silently lost.
3. If it looks good, run `deploy-to-github.ps1` — it reads `SheetPulse.html` directly and pushes it to GitHub as `index.html`. `TestLocal.html`/`TestLocal.bat` are never touched by the deploy script and never get pushed anywhere.

Testing locally works for Google Sign-In and live sheet data (the proxy's CORS and the Google OAuth client both additionally allow `http://localhost:8000` for this reason), but not for biometric quick-unlock — a WebAuthn credential is tied to its exact domain by design, so that one can only be tested on the real deployed URL.

### Confirming a deploy is byte-identical to what you tested

The header shows a line like `Build: 20/09/2026 16:43:31 · Content: a40b197c`. The two halves answer different questions:

- **Build** is the deploy timestamp (`BUILD_ID`, stamped fresh by `deploy-to-github.ps1` on every push). `TestLocal.html`/`SheetPulse.html` always show "local (not yet deployed)" since they're never stamped.
- **Content** is a short SHA-256 fingerprint of the page's own source, computed with the `BUILD_ID` line blanked out first — so it's independent of *when* something deployed and only reflects the actual bytes. If the `Content` hash in `TestLocal.html` matches the `Content` hash on the live GitHub Pages URL right after deploying, that proves the deploy pushed exactly what you tested, not just "something recent."

## Tech stack

- Plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript — no framework, no bundler.
- [D3.js](https://d3js.org/) for charts and the treemap.
- [PapaParse](https://www.papaparse.com/) for CSV parsing.
- [Google Identity Services](https://developers.google.com/identity/gsi/web) for Sign In With Google.
- A Node.js proxy (OAuth2 + Google Sheets API v4 + `google-auth-library` for ID token verification) behind Caddy for automatic HTTPS, self-hosted on a free-tier Oracle Cloud VM, reached at `api.sheetpulse.org` (domain and DNS at Spaceship).
- Hosted as a static page (e.g. GitHub Pages).
- A web app manifest (`manifest.json`) and a minimal service worker (`sw.js`) for PWA installability.
- The native browser WebAuthn API (`navigator.credentials`) on the frontend, and `@simplewebauthn/server` + `jsonwebtoken` on the proxy, for biometric quick-unlock.

## Privacy

No data is sent anywhere except from your browser to the private proxy, and from the proxy to Google's Sheets API. There is no analytics and no third party involved in loading your data. The spreadsheets themselves are not published to the web; access requires signing in with the one allowed Google account, which the proxy independently re-verifies on every request alongside an origin restriction. If you enable biometric unlock, your fingerprint/face data itself never leaves your device — only a public key is stored on the proxy, standard for WebAuthn.
